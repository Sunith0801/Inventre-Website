01

Summary and the decisions we need

Inventre holds personal data about roughly 15,000 families: parents' phone numbers, names and addresses; children's names, schools, grades, dates of birth and sizes; order and payment records; and, through the fee ledger, the fee and receipt history of about 12,000 students across eight school branches. Almost all of it concerns children. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act) Inventre is a Data Fiduciary for the storefront data and, for the fee ledger, acts on behalf of the schools.

Until 24 September 2026 nothing was deleted except expired carts and Redis keys. The Act requires erasure once the purpose is served, unless another law requires retention. Since 2026-09-24 a nightly job (/api/cron/data-retention, 02:40 IST) evaluates every rule below. Three rules act automatically; the rest only report the number of rows they would touch until the owner switches them on (RETENTION_APPLY=1). ?dry=1 previews all counts. 07

How it is protected (security measures)

In transit

  • TLS everywhere (Let's Encrypt), HSTS, security headers verified after every deploy
  • Partner links signed (HMAC) or encrypted (CCAvenue AES); replication and internal links inside a private Docker network

At rest

  • Passwords hashed with bcrypt; no card data ever stored
  • Backups encrypted on the server before leaving it; configuration encrypted inside snapshots
  • Database and Redis reachable only on the host; Redis password-protected

Access control

  • Four separate session types; role and per-page permissions; family-graph scoping for parents
  • Read-only support impersonation with edge enforcement
  • Key-only SSH, fail2ban, firewall on Docker ports

Accountability

  • Activity log for every admin change; OTP log; 5xx telemetry
  • Every production build carries its commit; a monitor detects a foreign build or lost security headers and e-mails
  • All production secrets rotated in September 2026 after the June compromise (database password pending)
Known weaknesses relevant to personal data

(1) Closed: OTP codes in otp_logs are now encrypted, and a reveal needs the key phrase and is logged (P-01). (2) Parents' photos stay on unguessable public links by owner decision, because the fulfilment ERP opens them directly; anyone with a link can read the photo (P-02). (3) Root access on the server sees everything, and one root compromise happened in June 2026; the remediation is in the HLD, the remaining item is the database password rotation (P-03). (4) Closed: the portal's photo upload now needs the ticket the search step issues, with per-IP limits (P-16). (5) Closed: a blocked parent loses their open session on the next request (P-17).

What the DPDP Act 2023 requires, and where we stand

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 is India's data-protection law. The Rules that bring most obligations into force were notified in November 2025 with a phased timeline; the substantive duties on fiduciaries apply from about eighteen months after notification. In practical terms Inventre should be compliant during 2027, and the work below takes months, so it starts now. Confirm the dates with counsel.

8.3 Privacy notice: what it must say (outline)

Published at /privacy on 2026-09-24 (version 2026-09-24) following this outline; awaiting counsel review.

  • Who we are and how to reach the grievance officer.
  • What we collect: parent contact details; child's name, school, grade, sizes; delivery address; order and payment records (never card details); photos you upload for an exchange.
  • Why: to show the right school kit, deliver it, invoice it, support you, and, for the fee console, to show the school's fee ledger on its behalf.
  • Who else sees it: the fulfilment team (Audit ERP), the payment gateway, the SMS provider, and Microsoft for e-mail and backups.
  • How long we keep it: the schedule in Section 6, in one sentence each.
  • Your rights: see, correct, delete, complain, nominate; how to do each; that withdrawing consent stops future processing but does not undo past orders we must keep for tax law.
  • For children: the parent account is the consent; how a parent removes a child.

Children's data

Every student record is a child's record. Section 9 of the Act adds three duties: obtain verifiable consent of the parent before processing a child's data, do not track or behaviourally monitor children, and do not direct targeted advertising at them. Inventre's design already fits well; the consent basis is written into the notice, the no-tracking test is built, and each child a parent links carries a consent record.

Breach response

The operational copy of this procedure, with the Board and parent notification templates, is docs/runbooks/breach-response.md. A personal-data breach is any unauthorised access, disclosure, loss or alteration of personal data. This is the procedure; it is short on purpose so it is followed under pressure.

WhenWhoDoes what
Hour 0Whoever notices (monitor e-mail, staff, parent, partner)Tells the owner and it@inventre.in. Nothing is deleted or rebooted before the Engineering lead has taken a snapshot (the 6-hourly snapshot script can be run by hand).
Hour 0–2Engineering leadContains: rotate the affected secret, revoke sessions if needed, block the source, put the maintenance page up if the storefront itself is compromised. Records a timeline in a shared note.
Hour 2–24Engineering lead + ownerScopes: which tables, how many families, which children's data, whether photos or fee data are involved. Uses the activity log, server logs and database queries. Decides whether personal data was actually exposed.
Within 72 hoursOwner (or grievance officer)Notifies the Data Protection Board in the prescribed form with the facts known so far, then the detailed report within the period the Rules set. Keeps counsel informed.
Without undue delayOwner + operations leadNotifies each affected parent (SMS or e-mail, in plain language): what happened, what data, what we did, what they should do (for example change their password), whom to contact. Informs the affected schools first.
Within 2 weeksEngineering leadRoot cause and fixes written up; this document and the HLD register updated; a drill scheduled.
Precedent

The June–July 2026 root compromise was contained (backdoor removed, key-only SSH enforced, watchdogs added, secrets rotated in September). No evidence of data exfiltration was found, but under the Act the same event today would require an assessment against the notification duty. This procedure exists so that assessment is fast and documented next time.
